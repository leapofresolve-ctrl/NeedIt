First: who is filtering, and why?

Worth being blunt about, because the answer governs every screen below.

The boardContains only demand — every row is a buyer saying "I want this."
Who filters itSellers. Filtering the board means "find demand I can fill." It is always a seller-side act.
How you buyBy posting a need — never by searching. There is no "search for cards to buy" on Exprifi. The day we add one, we've rebuilt eBay and thrown away the thesis.
Your own stuffNeeds you posted and offers you sent live on a dashboard, not as a filter of the board. Keeping those apart is most of what prevents this confusion.
The catchThe beachhead is buyer and seller in the same person. So the same account posts needs and filters the board — but never in the same surface. Two doors, always.

How the two surfaces divide, precisely:
  • Search = free text across need title and description. Matches player, set, brand, year, team. Nothing else. If someone types "under 500" it searches for that phrase and finds nothing — which is honest and fixable, not broken.
  • Rail = every structured field: type, sport, price band, closing window, offer state. Anything with a defined set of values lives here.
  • They compose with AND, both live in searchParams, and the header chips show the combined state — the dashed chip is the text query so it reads as a different kind of filter.
  • The header stays locked so a seller scanning 40 rows never scrolls away from their controls. It condenses to ~52px after 120px of scroll to keep the board the biggest thing on screen.
Option B — revised, deliberately weakened

Saved views — bookmarks, not matching

Originally this was "My hunts" with email alerts. That was a mistake and Kyle caught it — a standing search that emails you when matching demand appears is Lane 1 matching, given away free in the MVP. It would have gutted the M2 paid feature before it shipped. So the feature survives with its teeth pulled: saved views are passive bookmarks. They remember a filter state and show a badge when you're already on the site. Nothing ever leaves the platform. No email, no push, no "we found you a match."

The boundary rule, stated once: free = you come look. Paid (M2) = it comes to you. Anything that reaches out to a seller unprompted is Lane 1 and is not free.

What it's still good for: a breaker who checks the board every morning stops re-clicking the same four filters, and the "3 new" badge gives them a reason to open the tab. Real retention, zero risk to M2.
What it's now deliberately NOT good for — and that's the point: it will not tell you about demand you haven't come to look at. That gap is exactly what M2 sells. Seeding-sprint correction: I previously claimed hunts were the mechanism for waking sellers up. With alerts removed, that's now a manual founder job — Kyle emails people himself during seeding. That's the right answer at 0 needs anyway, and it doesn't need to be a product feature.

Answered: the rail is permanent

Two states you'll actually see first

The rail is always visible at ≥1024px — "Refine" is only the mobile presentation, never a desktop control. Hidden filters are unused filters, and the counts are demand data, not just chrome. But two states need designing: the escape hatch, and what happens before the board has earned a rail.

1 · Collapsed — the escape hatch

A "Hide filters" link at the foot of the rail, remembered in localStorage. The rail becomes a 44px ink tab clamped to the left edge; active filters stay in the header chips so nothing is lost. Twenty minutes of work that ends the argument.
