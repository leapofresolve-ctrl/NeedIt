Teaching new users — the empty state, generalised

Kyle's observation: the 0-needs landing state is already doing tutorial work. It has nothing to show, so it explains what the place is instead. The problem is that teaching disappears the moment the board fills — but new users don't. So: take the three ideas the empty state is carrying and give them a permanent home.

The three ideas — everything below is these, arranged differently

What actually needs teaching (and what doesn't)

Only one thing about Exprifi is genuinely unfamiliar: the direction. Posting, offering, accepting — everyone already understands those from every marketplace they've used. Don't teach what they know. Teach the flip, the two doors, and why it's safe to say out loud what you want.

01

You don't shop here

There's no catalogue to browse and nothing for sale. You post what you're after and sellers bring it to you.

“5,000ct of 90s basketball bulk, up to $420” → sellers compete for it.
02

Two doors, one account

Post a need when you want to buy. Browse the board when you want to sell — every row there is a buyer.

Most bulk guys use both. Same login, different door.
03

Saying what you want is safe

Offers are structured, not a public chat. Who you are stays hidden until a deal funds. Everything stays on Exprifi.

Nobody can see your want list and go buy it out from under you.
Why card 03 is not optional: a bulk reseller's whole edge is knowing what to look for. Asking them to post that publicly is asking them to leak it. Leak defence isn't a trust badge here — it's the thing that makes the product usable at all, so it has to be taught, not buried in a policy page.

Guardrail

What we are deliberately not building

✕ Not this
  • A modal carousel on first login
  • Pulsing coach-marks pointing at buttons
  • A 5-step "tour" with Next / Skip
  • A progress bar with a percentage
  • Confetti, badges, streaks
  • "You haven't finished setup!" emails
  • A video anyone has to watch
✓ This
  • Teach in place, over the real board
  • One sentence at the moment it's needed
  • Everything dismissible, nothing modal
  • A permanent public page anyone can re-read
  • Plain language, ink, no accent colour
  • On-site only — never reaches out
  • Learns by doing, then gets out of the way
Why the anti-list matters more than usual here: the beachhead is 45+ and allergic to feeling handled. A tour that blocks the screen reads as a startup demo; a quiet line of text next to the thing it describes reads as a business that thought about you. It's the same "official = restraint + furniture" principle from the 3b pass, applied to teaching.
