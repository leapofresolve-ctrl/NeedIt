# Company Overview

## One-line pitch
**Exprifi — demand becomes the listing.** A reverse marketplace for sports cards: buyers post the card or lot they want, and sellers race to fill it.

## Mission
Flip the marketplace: make purchase intent — not inventory — the unit of commerce, so every collector gets what they want at their price, and every seller gets a live feed of buyers with money in hand.

## Vision (Kyle's words)
"You know how Amazon started with books and became the giant it is? That's how I look at Exprifi. I want to top eBay." Sports cards are the books. The reverse-marketplace mechanic generalizes to every collectible vertical (Disney, TCG, and beyond) once the sports-card beachhead is won.

## The differentiation thesis (the wedge)
The lead differentiator is the **demand-side model** — everything else (fees, trust, niche, AI) is supporting cast. Kyle's ranking is explicit: demand-side model first; the other identities feel oversaturated in the current market.

**Why a buyer chooses Exprifi over eBay:** near-certainty their want gets filled *at their price*. eBay is full of cards listed way above comp ($20 cards sitting at $40+ because stock is thin). On Exprifi you skip the fluff and the endless searching — deals come to you with photos, you pay quickly. The time saved is worth a 5% fee on single-card deals.

**Why a seller chooses Exprifi over eBay:** at any moment, the exact card they've been trying to move can pop up on the want board with a funded buyer attached — a global audience instead of their local network. Sellers pay **zero fees** and keep 100% (buyer carries the 5% on singles), which drives sellers to compete to fulfill, which keeps buyers happy. (See fees-and-monetization.md — **RESOLVED Jul 27, 2026**; superseded Aug 2, 2026 to apply the 5% to all single-card deals rather than only high-end singles. No fee on bulk lots or filter requests, ever.)

**The moat:** if Exprifi owns the demand graph of the hobby — who wants what, at what price, right now — that data plus Lane 1 (instant price-match engine) becomes something no listing site can copy by adding a feature.

## Where the failure is worst today
Bulk / low-end / "treasure hunt" deals. You cannot search eBay for "a 5,000-count box of 2019–21 basketball with some rookies." Those deals happen in Facebook groups, IG DMs, and live breaks — chaotic, trust-poor, unsearchable. That's the beachhead.

## Endgame
Build it like Portnoy built Barstool: live the CEO life, pour everything in, no quick flip — then sell when it's time. Acquisition (plausibly by eBay itself) is the transparent end goal. Success also has a personal definition: paying off college for "all my boys from home."

## Strategy skeleton (locked)
- **Lane 2 (BUILT, live):** open request board — needs + structured offers. MVP.
- **Lane 1 (M2):** instant price-match against seller inventory — the moat. Earned only after Lane 2 proves liquidity.
- **M3:** payments/escrow (Stripe — **test mode only** per the Jul 27, 2026 replan; goes live only after the Sep 14 liquidity gate passes).
- **M4:** monetization on.
